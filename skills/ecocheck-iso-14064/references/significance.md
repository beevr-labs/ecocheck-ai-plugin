# Significance of indirect emissions (categories 3-6)

ISO 14064-1:2018 asks the organization to define its own, documented criteria to decide which indirect emissions are significant, apply them consistently, and justify every exclusion. Direct emissions (category 1) and imported energy (category 2) are always reported.

## Criteria commonly used (pick, weight and document)

| Criterion | Question | Example scoring |
|---|---|---|
| Magnitude | Estimated share of the total inventory | ≥ 5 % = 3, 1-5 % = 2, < 1 % = 1 |
| Level of influence | Can the organization reduce it (supplier choice, design, logistics)? | High 3 / medium 2 / low 1 |
| Risk or opportunity | Regulatory (CBAM, customer requirements), reputational, financial exposure | High 3 / medium 2 / low 1 |
| Sector guidance | Is it material for the sector (e.g. purchased steel for a fabricator)? | Yes 3 / partly 2 / no 1 |
| Outsourcing | Is it an activity typically done in-house by peers? | Yes 3 / no 1 |
| Stakeholder interest | Customers, investors, employees ask for it | High 3 / low 1 |

Rule of thumb: include a source when its weighted score reaches the documented threshold, or when magnitude alone is ≥ the threshold (e.g. 5 %). Re-run the assessment when the business changes.

## Screening steps

1. List every plausible indirect source (see categories-and-boundaries.md).
2. Screen magnitude with spend-based or average factors (rough is fine at this stage; label it "screening").
3. Score each source on the criteria; record evidence (spend, tonnes, km).
4. Decide include / exclude; write the justification for exclusions in the report.
5. For included sources, plan better data (supplier-specific > average-data > spend-based).
