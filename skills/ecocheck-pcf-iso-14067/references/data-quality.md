# Data quality, uncertainty, sensitivity and scenarios

## Primary and secondary data

- **Primary (site-specific)**: measured or calculated from the company's own records or the supplier's own footprint for that product — meters, invoices, production reports, a supplier PCF.
- **Secondary**: averages from databases, national statistics, literature.
- Use primary data for the processes the company runs and, as far as possible, for the inputs that dominate the footprint. Report the **share of primary data** (by emissions) — PACT footprints and many customers ask for it.

## Data quality ratings (PEF-style, 1 = very good … 5 = very poor)

Rate each significant input on:

| Criterion | 1 (very good) | 3 (fair) | 5 (very poor) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technological representativeness (TeR) | the same technology | similar technology | different technology |
| Geographical representativeness (GeR) | the same country / grid | same region | another continent / global average |
| Time representativeness (TiR) | same year as the study | within ~3-6 years | older than ~10 years |

A weighted average over the inputs (weights = their share of the footprint) gives an overall rating: up to 1.6 excellent / very good, up to 2 very good, up to 3 good, up to 4 fair, above 4 poor (state the scale you use).

## Uncertainty

- Give each important input an uncertainty (± %, distribution) from the data source or an expert judgement; run a Monte Carlo simulation and report the 95 % interval and the coefficient of variation.
- Report which inputs drive the uncertainty (rank correlation) — they are where better data pays off.
- Qualitative statement when no quantitative data exist: list the main sources of uncertainty and their direction.

## Sensitivity

- Change each parameter (quantity, distance, recycled content, electricity factor) by a fixed share (e.g. ± 10 %) and record the change in the footprint; present a tornado ranking.
- Test methodological choices too: allocation method, recycling approach, grid vs contractual electricity, AR5 vs AR6.

## Scenarios and ecodesign

- A scenario is a named set of parameter values (recycled content 30 % vs 60 %, supplier A vs B, sea vs road transport). Compare scenarios with the baseline per declared unit.
- For design variants with a different structure, copy the product (variant) instead of using parameters, and compare the products side by side (first = base).
- When comparing products publicly (comparative assertions), ISO 14067 / ISO 14044 require extra conditions (same functional unit, critical review); keep internal comparisons internal.
